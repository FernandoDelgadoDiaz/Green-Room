Speak to three people: Jason Calacanis (podcaster and investor), Cedric the Entertainer (host, Qloo backer), Alex Elias (Qloo CEO). Keep the app open on a show they know (This Week in Startups or All-In) before you start.

## The 2-minute pitch (English)

**0:00 · Hook (15 s)**
> "Every podcast lives or dies by its guests, and most shows book them the same way: from memory, from their own circle, from whoever's famous this week. Green Room is an agent that books guests on evidence and on taste."

**0:15 · Live demo (60 s)**: open *This Week in Startups*.
> "Here's This Week in Startups. Green Room read its real episode history and found who Jason actually interviews: founders, investors, operators, mostly American.
> These are five guests he hasn't had. Each one has real interviews on comparable shows, with links, and they're the kind of guest this audience knows.
> And this is where Qloo comes in. For each guest, Qloo tells us how strongly this audience has affinity for them. And up here, Qloo describes who this show's audience is."

Point at: a card's green "Qloo on this guest's audience" strip (affinity), then "Who this show's audience is" (e.g. All-In: skews 24 and younger and 30–34, mostly men).

**1:15 · Why only Qloo (25 s)**
> "Your RSS feed tells you who you booked. Only Qloo tells you what your audience likes. Without Qloo, Green Room can list credible people. With Qloo, it can tell a producer whose audience is yours and who your audience is. It also finds people your own history never had."

**1:40 · Who it's for, and the close (20 s)**
> "It works for any show in Apple's directory and in five countries, so an Argentine show gets Argentine guests. Producers, booking agencies and networks are buying a defensible shortlist in seconds instead of a week of research. Qloo finds the taste. Green Room checks it against public evidence. The producer decides."

## Likely questions, short answers

**"How do you make money?"**
A subscription for producers and networks: per show, with weekly shortlists and invitation drafts. Agencies and networks pay per seat. Later: a booking marketplace where guests' teams see shows whose audiences match them.

**"Isn't this just ChatGPT?"**
No language model ranks anyone. A rule-based agent works from public evidence (RSS interview history, Wikidata identity and country) and from Qloo's taste graph, and every step says why it ran. An LLM can't tell you whose audience overlaps yours; Qloo can.

**"Does Qloo actually change the picks?"**
Qloo affinity reorders comparable candidates, and Qloo discovers people the show's history never had. We were careful: in historical backtests, Qloo didn't raise hit rates, so we don't claim better predictions. What Qloo adds is the audience read: who the show’s audience is by age and gender, and how much affinity it has for each guest. That is what a producer needs to defend a booking.

**"How do you know who the audience is?"**
We don't, and we say so. Qloo gives aggregate taste and demographic affinity for the show's signals. It's inferred, never listener data, and it's labeled that way.

**"What about people who aren't famous?"**
Identity is confirmed with context, never by name alone. Every guest must have a verifiable record, and nobody deceased, nobody already on the show, and never the host.

**"What's next?"**
Spotify and YouTube shows, which are most of Latin American podcasting. Outreach drafts in the producer's voice. Alerts when a matching guest starts trending on Qloo.

**"Who built it?"**
Fernando Delgado, from Argentina. Say one line about yourself and why podcasts.
